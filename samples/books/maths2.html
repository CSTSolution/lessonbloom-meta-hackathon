Lesson 20: Adding without regrouping within 100

Explore. There are 23 orange trees and 14 lemon trees in the garden. To find how many trees there are in all, we add 23 + 14.

Learn. Write the numbers in columns: ones under ones, tens under tens. Add from right to left: first the ones, then the tens.

Example. 23 + 14: 3 plus 4 is 7, write 7. 2 plus 1 is 3, write 3. So 23 + 14 = 37.

Practice. Work out: 32 + 15; 41 + 26; 50 + 37; 64 + 23.

Use it. Class 2A has 21 girls and 16 boys. How many children are there in Class 2A?

Lesson 21: Adding with regrouping within 100

Explore. Mia has 36 apples. Her mum gives her 27 more apples. To find how many apples Mia has in all, we add 36 + 27.

Learn. When the ones add up to 10 or more, write the ones digit and carry 1 to the tens. When you add the tens, remember to add the 1 you carried.

Example. 36 + 27: 6 plus 7 is 13, write 3 and carry 1. 3 plus 2 is 5, plus 1 is 6, write 6. So 36 + 27 = 63.

Example 2. 48 + 25 = 73. 8 plus 5 is 13, write 3 and carry 1; 4 plus 2 is 6, plus 1 is 7.

Practice. Work out: 19 + 36; 57 + 28; 44 + 39; 26 + 47.

Use it. A shop sold 38 kg of rice in the morning and 45 kg of rice in the afternoon. How many kilograms of rice did the shop sell in the whole day?

Watch out. Children often forget to carry 1 to the tens, for example writing 36 + 27 = 53.

Lesson 22: Subtracting without regrouping within 100

Explore. There are 58 birds on a tree. 23 birds fly away. To find how many birds are left, we subtract 58 − 23.

Learn. Write the numbers in columns. Subtract from right to left: first the ones, then the tens.

Example. 58 − 23: 8 minus 3 is 5, write 5. 5 minus 2 is 3, write 3. So 58 − 23 = 35.

Practice. Work out: 47 − 15; 69 − 34; 86 − 52; 95 − 41.

Use it. Tom had 76 stickers. He gave 35 stickers to his friend. How many stickers does Tom have left?

Lesson 23: Subtracting with regrouping within 100

Explore. A farmer has 52 eggs. He sells 28 eggs. To find how many eggs are left, we subtract 52 − 28.

Learn. When the ones digit on top is smaller, take 1 ten from the tens and make 10 more ones. Then subtract the ones, and subtract the tens from what is left.

Example. 52 − 28: 2 is smaller than 8, so take 1 ten: 12 minus 8 is 4, write 4. 5 minus 1 is 4, 4 minus 2 is 2, write 2. So 52 − 28 = 24.

Practice. Work out: 61 − 37; 83 − 49; 74 − 26; 90 − 45.

Use it. A library had 64 books on a shelf. Children borrowed 29 books. How many books are left on the shelf?

Watch out. Children often take the smaller digit from the bigger one, for example writing 52 − 28 = 36.

Lesson 24: Word problems

Learn. Read the problem slowly. Find the numbers and what the question asks. Decide: do we put together (add) or take away (subtract)? Write the calculation and the answer with its unit.

Example. Anna picked 34 flowers and Ben picked 18 flowers. How many flowers did they pick in all? 34 + 18 = 52. They picked 52 flowers.

Practice. A bus had 45 people. 17 people got off. How many people are still on the bus? A box has 28 red pencils and 35 blue pencils. How many pencils are in the box?
